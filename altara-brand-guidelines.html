01 — Logo

The Mark

The Altara monogram pairs a carved, arch-like "A" with a diamond lattice motif — a nod to Himalayan textile and woodwork patterns — resting above the classic serif wordmark. It should always read as quiet, carved, and permanent, never decorative or busy.

Primary Lockup — On Light
On Dark — Recommended Fix Below
Flagged for review: the source file supplied is a single dark-green/gold lockup on a transparent-to-white ground. On the deep green (#16302A) and near-black (#0E1512) surfaces used throughout the site, this version loses most of its contrast. A true reversed lockup — cream or white "A" and wordmark, gold diamond motif retained — should be produced as a companion asset before the mark is used on any dark background in production. Treat the dark-panel example above as a placeholder showing the problem, not an approved usage.

Clearspace & Minimum Size

Give the mark room to breathe. Never let text, imagery, or edges intrude on its clearspace, and never scale it below legibility.

Minimum clearspace on all sides = the height of the diamond motif cluster at the center of the mark, measured at the lockup's current proportions.

Digital — 120px min width
Digital — 72px absolute floor
Below 48px — use mark only, drop wordmark

Don't

✕ Don't Recolor

Never apply colors outside the approved palette to the mark.

✕ Don't Distort

Never stretch, skew, or disproportionately scale the lockup.

✕ Don't Rotate

The mark is always presented perfectly upright.

✕ Don't Bury It

Never place it on busy imagery or low-contrast backgrounds.

04 — Voice & Tone

How Altara Speaks

Quiet confidence, not salesmanship. Altara writes the way a trusted guide speaks — specific, unhurried, and never over-explaining.

Unhurried

Sentences have room to breathe. We don't rush to the CTA — we earn it with specificity.

Specific, Not Superlative

"Never more than eight travellers" beats "an unforgettable experience." Concrete details carry the luxury, not adjectives.

Warm, Not Familiar

Respectful distance. We're a trusted guide, not a friend texting — no exclamation points, no forced enthusiasm.

Rooted in Place

Copy names real things — a valley, a family, a pass — rather than abstractions like "paradise" or "adventure."

Altara says

"We spend the months you don't see: securing the guide who trained a generation of trekkers."

Not this

"Get ready for the trip of a lifetime! Our AMAZING guides can't wait to show you paradise!"
