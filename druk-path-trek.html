Druk Path Trek Overview

The Druk Path Trek is Bhutan's most accessible high-altitude trek — a compact route linking Paro and Thimphu on foot, over a chain of ridgelines and alpine lakes, without the long approach a trek like Jomolhari or the Snowman requires.

Over six days, the trail climbs from Paro through blue pine and rhododendron forest to the ridge at Jele Dzong, then follows a string of high pastures and lakes — Jangchulakha, Jimilang Tsho, Simkotra Tsho — with Mount Jomolhari visible on a clear day from Phume La, the trek's highest point.

The route descends past Phajoding Monastery, one of Bhutan's largest monastic communities, into Thimphu itself — arriving in the capital on foot rather than by road. This is a fully supported camping trek, with a dedicated crew of guides, cooks and pack animals.

Trek Highlights

  • Panoramic Himalayan views, including Mount Jomolhari, from Phume La (4,210m)
  • A string of high alpine lakes: Jangchulakha, Jimilang Tsho and Simkotra Tsho
  • The ridge-top ruins of Jele Dzong above the Paro valley
  • Rhododendron and blue pine forest along the trail
  • A descent through Phajoding Monastery into Thimphu on foot
  • A fully supported camp with dedicated cook and trek crew
Day By Day

The Itinerary

Land at Paro's mountain-ringed airport, settle into your hotel and meet your trek crew for a gear check and route briefing over dinner.

From near Ta Dzong, climb steadily through blue pine forest to camp at the ridge-top ruins of Jele Dzong, with your first views back over the Paro valley.

A ridge walk through rhododendron forest and yak pastures, with Mount Jomolhari visible on a clear day.

Descend to camp beside Jimilang Tsho, the trek's largest lake, known locally for its giant trout.

Cross the trek's high point at Phume La (4,210m), pass a second alpine lake, then descend to camp near Phajoding Monastery above Thimphu.

A final descent into the capital on foot, before your private transfer onward.

What To Pack For The Druk Path Trek

Nights above 3,700m can drop well below freezing even in peak season, so layering matters more than any single piece of gear. Pack a warm sleeping bag rated to at least -5°C, a down jacket, waterproof shell, thermal base layers, sturdy broken-in hiking boots, trekking poles, a headlamp, sun protection and a basic personal first-aid kit. Your trek crew carries tents, kitchen equipment and food, so you'll only need a daypack for the walking day itself.
